🧳 Luggage strategy for Day 1 — bringing bags on the train (Yamato won't deliver in time for Friday night)

Decision made: bringing all 6 suitcases with you on the Skyliner + JR + Tobu route. Yamato is off the table because next-day delivery means bags wouldn't arrive at the Airbnb until ~6 PM Saturday — too late. Cost: ~$110 in train fares for 6 ppl. Below is how to make the bags-on-trains experience as painless as possible.

Tactical tips for bags on this route:

  • Skyliner is fine — it's a reserved-seat airport train with luggage racks at both ends of each car. Get in early to claim rack space; in a pinch, your bag rides at your feet.
  • Nippori transfer (3-5 min walk + stairs) — there ARE elevators between Keisei platforms and JR platforms; follow the lift signs (a wheelchair icon). Slower than escalators but spares everyone the suitcase-on-stairs juggle.
  • JR Joban Rapid — only a 6-min ride. Stand near the doorway with bags upright; locals expect tourists with luggage at this hour.
  • Kita-Senju transfer (5–10 min) — large station, multiple line transfers. Use the elevators (signed in English). Allow the full 10 min to avoid rushing.
  • Tobu Skytree Line 1 stop = 3 min, easy.
  • Senju-Ohashi exit → Airbnb — split the group here, see the step-by-step below. Lala/Aileen/Ella take a cab; the boys (Ryan, Reid, Raiden) walk with their bags.

Backup if it gets ugly: if at any point the transfers are too rough (e.g., elevators broken, rush hour hellish), bail to a taxi from whichever station you're at — Tokyo cabs are everywhere and fares are reasonable. Worst case: 2 cabs from Nippori → Airbnb is ~¥6,000-8,000 (~$40-55 total) and takes ~30 min. Keep that as your "abort plan."

If anyone wants to reconsider before the trip: the Keisei Airport Limousine Bus (Narita → Kita-Senju Marui direct, ~90 min, ¥1,800/pp + bags in cargo hold) is still on the table for ~$140 total — gets bags to the Airbnb same evening with zero train transfers. But if "bring bags on trains" is the call, the playbook above is the way.

Nearest options to the Airbnb (search Google Maps as you walk over):

🚆 Transit & Navigation Cheat Sheet

Read this once before your trip and the rest of the itinerary's transit shorthand will make sense. The Japanese train system looks complex but is just lots of named lines stacked together — once you know the line names, every direction in this doc reads like a recipe.

📲 Step 1: Google Maps is your friend

For every transit instruction in this itinerary, you can plug the destination into Google Maps on your phone. It tells you exact platform numbers, live departure times, walking directions inside stations, and the fare. This is your primary navigation tool — every link in this doc opens Google Maps with the destination already loaded. Backups: Apple Maps (surprisingly good in Japan) and Japan Travel by NAVITIME (Japanese-tourist-focused, shows alternate routes).

💳 Step 2: Suica is your magic tap-card

Suica (or PASMO — same thing, different brand) is a rechargeable plastic card or phone wallet card. Tap it on the gate at every station, and you never need to buy individual tickets for local trains. Buy one at any train station ticket machine for ¥2,000 (¥1,500 of that loads as travel value, ¥500 deposit). Top up at any machine with cash. Works on EVERY line below. Even better: add Mobile Suica to Apple Wallet before you fly (iPhone XS+) — then your phone is the card.

🚉 Step 3: The lines we'll actually use

Japan's transit is organized by line names, not by destination. When the itinerary says "Tobu Skytree Line direct, 10 min," it means: get on the Tobu Skytree Line, ride it 10 min without transferring, get off at the named station. Here's a decoder for every line in this doc:

JR (Japan Railways) — the national rail network

  • JR Yamanote Line (green) — Tokyo's iconic loop. Connects Tokyo Sta., Ueno, Akihabara, Shinjuku, Shibuya. You'll ride this constantly.
  • JR Joban Line — runs north from Ueno through Kita-Senju (your Tokyo transit hub for the Senju-Ohashi Airbnb).
  • JR Keiyō Line — Tokyo Sta. → Maihama (Disney parks). Used Day 4.
  • JR Sagano Line (also called JR Sanin Line) — Kyoto Sta. → Arashiyama. Used Day 6.
  • JR Nara Line — Kyoto Sta. → Fushimi-Inari. Used Day 6 morning (alternative to Keihan).

Tokyo Metro (the main subway)

  • Hibiya Line (silver "H") — runs Naka-Meguro ↔ Kita-Senju, hits Akihabara, Tsukiji, Hibiya. Your most-used subway from the Senju-Ohashi base.
  • Ginza Line (orange "G") — oldest subway in Asia. Connects Asakusa ↔ Ueno ↔ Shibuya. Used for Asakusa trips.
  • Chiyoda Line (green "C") — runs through Kita-Senju, Otemachi (Tokyo Sta. area), Omote-Sandō.
  • Marunouchi Line (red "M") — Tokyo Sta. ↔ Shinjuku.

Toei Subway (Tokyo's other municipal subway, separate from Metro)

  • Toei Asakusa Line (rose pink "A") — Asakusa Sta. → Narita Airport via Keisei Sky Access. Your Day 8 departure route.

Tobu — a private (non-JR, non-Metro) railway

  • Tobu Skytree Line — runs from Asakusa Station up through Senju-Ohashi (your Airbnb's station) and onward to the suburbs. Your direct 10-minute ride between Senju-Ohashi and Asakusa with no transfers. "Tobu" (東武, "East-Side Railway") is just the company name. Suica works on Tobu the same as on JR/Metro.

Keisei (KAY-say) — private railway connecting Tokyo to Narita Airport

  • Keisei Skyliner — premium nonstop airport train. Narita ↔ Nippori/Keisei Ueno in ~40 min. Reserved seats, comfortable, ¥2,580/pp. Velasquez Day 1 arrival route.
  • Keisei Access Express — cheaper express that runs Asakusa ↔ Narita with no transfers via the Toei Asakusa Line. ¥1,310/pp, ~67 min. Day 8 group departure route.

Keihan (KAY-han) — private railway in Kyoto/Osaka

  • Keihan Main Line — runs Kyoto (Demachi-yanagi/Sanjō/Gion-Shijō/Kiyomizu-Gojō/Shichijō) all the way to Osaka (Yodoyabashi/Temmabashi). Direct service from your Kyoto Airbnb to both Fushimi Inari (Day 6 sunrise) and Osaka (Day 5). No transfers needed for either trip.

Other lines mentioned

  • Tokyu Toyoko Line — Shibuya ↔ Yokohama. Used once on Day 3 if you do the Wagyumafia detour to Naka-Meguro.
  • Hankyu Arashiyama Line — Kyoto's other private line, fallback option for Arashiyama on Day 6 if you don't taxi.
  • Shinkansen (新幹線) — Japan's bullet train. Tōkaidō Shinkansen runs Tokyo ↔ Kyoto. Reserved seats via SmartEX app, ~2h 40min, ~$95/pp each way.
