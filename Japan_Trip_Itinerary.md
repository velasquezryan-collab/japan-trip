**Tactical tips for buying luxury used in Tokyo:**

  - **Authenticity:** all major chains (Ragtag, Komehyo, ALLU, Vintage Qoo, Amore) authenticate every piece — the Tokyo market is famously trusted. Counterfeit risk is near-zero at these shops.
  - **Tax-free shopping:** bring your **passport** — purchases over ¥5,000 (\~$31) qualify for **10% tax-free** as a tourist. Show passport at checkout.
  - **Best for:** Hermès Birkin/Kelly (often 20–40% below US/EU retail), Rolex sport models (huge inventory), Chanel classic flap (deeper discounts than US), Louis Vuitton bags from prior seasons.
  - **Cards:** all chains accept Visa/Amex/Mastercard. Bring an unlocked credit card with no foreign transaction fees + a high credit limit — these purchases can hit ceilings fast.
  - **Time required:** Ragtag/ALLU = \~30 min each · Vintage Qoo = \~30 min · Komehyo Shinjuku = 60–90 min (it's HUGE).

## 🚆 Transit & Navigation Cheat Sheet

Read this once before your trip and the rest of the itinerary's transit shorthand will make sense. The Japanese train system looks complex but is just lots of named lines stacked together — once you know the line names, every direction in this doc reads like a recipe.

### 📲 Step 1: Google Maps is your friend

For every transit instruction in this itinerary, you can plug the destination into **Google Maps** on your phone. It tells you exact platform numbers, live departure times, walking directions inside stations, and the fare. **This is your primary navigation tool — every link in this doc opens Google Maps with the destination already loaded.** Backups: [Apple Maps](https://maps.apple.com/) (surprisingly good in Japan) and [Japan Travel by NAVITIME](https://japantravel.navitime.com/) (Japanese-tourist-focused, shows alternate routes).

### 💳 Step 2: Suica is your magic tap-card

**Suica** (or PASMO — same thing, different brand) is a rechargeable plastic card or phone wallet card. **Tap it on the gate at every station, and you never need to buy individual tickets for local trains.** Buy one at any train station ticket machine for ¥2,000 (¥1,500 of that loads as travel value, ¥500 deposit). Top up at any machine with cash. Works on EVERY line below. Even better: add **Mobile Suica to Apple Wallet before you fly** (iPhone XS+) — then your phone is the card.

### 🚉 Step 3: The lines we'll actually use

Japan's transit is organized by **line names**, not by destination. When the itinerary says "Tobu Skytree Line direct, 10 min," it means: get on the Tobu Skytree Line, ride it 10 min without transferring, get off at the named station. Here's a decoder for every line in this doc:

**JR (Japan Railways) — the national rail network**

  - **JR Yamanote Line (green)** — Tokyo's iconic loop. Connects Tokyo Sta., Ueno, Akihabara, Shinjuku, Shibuya. You'll ride this constantly.
  - **JR Joban Line** — runs north from Ueno through **Kita-Senju** (your Tokyo transit hub for the Senju-Ohashi Airbnb).
  - **JR Keiyō Line** — Tokyo Sta. → Maihama (Disney parks). Used Day 4.
  - **JR Sagano Line** (also called JR Sanin Line) — Kyoto Sta. → Arashiyama. Used Day 6.
  - **JR Nara Line** — Kyoto Sta. → Fushimi-Inari. Used Day 6 morning (alternative to Keihan).

**Tokyo Metro (the main subway)**

  - **Hibiya Line (silver "H")** — runs Naka-Meguro ↔ Kita-Senju, hits Akihabara, Tsukiji, Hibiya. **Your most-used subway from the Senju-Ohashi base.**
  - **Ginza Line (orange "G")** — oldest subway in Asia. Connects Asakusa ↔ Ueno ↔ Shibuya. Used for Asakusa trips.
  - **Chiyoda Line (green "C")** — runs through Kita-Senju, Otemachi (Tokyo Sta. area), Omote-Sandō.
  - **Marunouchi Line (red "M")** — Tokyo Sta. ↔ Shinjuku.

**Toei Subway (Tokyo's other municipal subway, separate from Metro)**

  - **Toei Asakusa Line (rose pink "A")** — Asakusa Sta. → Narita Airport via Keisei Sky Access. **Your Day 8 departure route.**

**Tobu — a private (non-JR, non-Metro) railway**

  - **Tobu Skytree Line** — runs from Asakusa Station up through **Senju-Ohashi** (your Airbnb's station) and onward to the suburbs. **Your direct 10-minute ride between Senju-Ohashi and Asakusa with no transfers.** "Tobu" (東武, "East-Side Railway") is just the company name. *Suica works on Tobu the same as on JR/Metro.*

**Keisei (KAY-say) — private railway connecting Tokyo to Narita Airport**

  - **Keisei Skyliner** — premium nonstop airport train. Narita ↔ Nippori/Keisei Ueno in \~40 min. Reserved seats, comfortable, ¥2,580/pp. **Velasquez Day 1 arrival route.**
  - **Keisei Access Express** — cheaper express that runs Asakusa ↔ Narita with no transfers via the Toei Asakusa Line. ¥1,310/pp, \~67 min. **Day 8 group departure route.**

**Keihan (KAY-han) — private railway in Kyoto/Osaka**

  - **Keihan Main Line** — runs Kyoto (Demachi-yanagi/Sanjō/Gion-Shijō/Kiyomizu-Gojō/Shichijō) all the way to Osaka (Yodoyabashi/Temmabashi). **Direct service from your Kyoto Airbnb to both Fushimi Inari (Day 6 sunrise) and Osaka (Day 5).** No transfers needed for either trip.

**Other lines mentioned**

  - **Tokyu Toyoko Line** — Shibuya ↔ Yokohama. Used once on Day 3 if you do the Wagyumafia detour to Naka-Meguro.
  - **Hankyu Arashiyama Line** — Kyoto's other private line, fallback option for Arashiyama on Day 6 if you don't taxi.
  - **Shinkansen (新幹線)** — Japan's bullet train. Tōkaidō Shinkansen runs Tokyo ↔ Kyoto. Reserved seats via SmartEX app, \~2h 40min, \~$95/pp each way.

### 🧭 Step 4: How to read every transit instruction in this doc

When you see something like:

Tobu to Kita-Senju 1 stop / 3 min + Hibiya Line Kita-Senju → Akihabara direct / 12 min · total \~20 min

…read it as a step-by-step recipe:

1.  Walk to **Senju-Ohashi Station** (the one nearest your Airbnb)
2.  Tap your Suica on the gate · take the **Tobu Skytree Line southbound** (toward Asakusa)
3.  Ride 1 stop (3 min) and get off at **Kita-Senju Station**
4.  Inside Kita-Senju, follow signs to the **Hibiya Line** (silver-colored "H"). Stations show a color + letter for every line. There may be a 2–5 min walk between platforms.
5.  Tap your Suica again on the Hibiya Line gates · take the train toward Naka-Meguro (this is the southbound direction toward Akihabara)
6.  Ride directly (no transfer) about 12 min to **Akihabara Station**
7.  Tap out · you've arrived

*Total trip: \~20 min including transfer time. Just open Google Maps for the live, station-specific details.*

### 🧳 Luggage Strategy: Yamato Forwarding (your secret weapon)

The single biggest quality-of-life upgrade for a family with multiple suitcases in Japan: **Yamato Transport luggage forwarding** (everyone calls it "Yamato" or its retail brand "**TA-Q-BIN**" — pronounced "TAH-cue-bin"). You drop your suitcases at a Yamato counter, they deliver to your next address overnight, and you ride trains with just a backpack. **Recommended for every major lodging move on this trip** — Day 1 Narita arrival, Day 5 Tokyo→Kyoto, Day 7 Kyoto→Tokyo.

**Why it matters for this group of 12:** Each adult brings a checked roller suitcase + backpack. Lugging 12 suitcases through Nippori transfer + Kita-Senju transfer + the 9-min residential walk to the Senju-Ohashi Airbnb at the end of a 12-hour flight is brutal — for everyone, but especially Lala (81). Yamato eliminates this entirely.

**How it works:**

1.  Drop your suitcase at a **Yamato counter** with the destination address (in Japanese ideally — your Airbnb host can email it to you)
2.  Pay ¥2,000–2,500 per bag (\~$13–16) for next-day delivery, \~¥3,500 (\~$22) for in-Tokyo same-day
3.  Get a tracking slip — your bag arrives at your next lodging by \~6 PM the next day (or \~6 PM same day for Tokyo→Tokyo)
4.  Travel hands-free with just a backpack containing valuables, 1 change of clothes, toiletries, meds

**Where to drop bags on this trip:**

  - **Narita Airport** — Yamato counter on the Arrivals floor, after customs (look for orange "TA-Q-BIN" / "Yamato Transport" sign). Open \~7 AM – 9 PM. Counter agents speak basic English. [Yamato at Narita →](https://www.kuronekoyamato.co.jp/en/airport/narita/)
